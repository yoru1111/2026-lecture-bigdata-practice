# Observations

## Task 1
R3: an empty cluster keeps its previous centroid (the same rule as `_means` in Task 3). On the 9 blob points this happened in 3 of 40 random seeds (22, 23, 37), and all three ended stuck at SSE 96.12 with only two real clusters. The alternative — move the empty centroid to the point furthest from its own centroid — rescued all three to SSE 0.12, raising random init from 24/40 to 27/40 correct; I kept "keep old" because it never invents a centroid and matches the baseline, but it is clearly the less forgiving rule.
Over 40 seeds, random initialisation found the three blobs 24 times (SSE 0.12) and failed 16 times (SSE ≈ 96, two blobs merged, one split); k-means++ found them 40/40. Mean SSE: random 38.5, ++ 0.12.
k-means++ costs about n·(k−1) extra distances before the first iteration (if you cache each point's nearest-so-far distance) — on the Task 3 bench that is 20,000 × 11 = 220,000, roughly one more full iteration — and buys starting centroids that are spread out, so far fewer bad local optima and usually fewer iterations. I would skip it when I already have good starting centroids (e.g. last run's, warm-start) or when n is so large that even one extra pass over the data is the expensive part. SSE cannot choose k because it always falls as k grows (here 256.1, 96.1, 0.12, 0.09, … 0.00 at k = 9, one point per cluster); the right k is where it stops falling sharply (the elbow at k = 3), not where it is smallest.

## Task 2
At my sample size (1,000–2,000 points, 20,000 sampled pairs) contrast dropped below 1 at d ≈ 50 (0.98) and below 0.5 between d = 100 and 200; mean distance grew as √(d/6) (ratio 0.99–1.00 from d = 10 up), and relative sd fell as ≈ 0.6/√d.
A6: n matters for contrast but not for relative sd — at d = 20 contrast ranged 1.73–2.67 as I changed n and the number of pairs (more distinct pairs → more extreme min and max), while relative sd stayed 0.134–0.135. So "the dimension where distance stops meaning anything" depends on how much data you have, and contrast is a noisy, sample-size-dependent way to measure it.
A8: I don't think it threatens week 3's LSH. Shingle sets are sparse and structured, near-duplicates keep high Jaccard however large the shingle vocabulary is, and MinHash's collision probability equals Jaccard regardless of dimension. It would only bite on a collection with no real near-duplicates, where all similarities bunch near 0.

## Task 3
I used Hamerly's bounds: per point one upper bound (distance to own centroid) and one lower bound (distance to the nearest other centroid) — 2 extra floats per point, 40,000 for n = 20,000 — plus per iteration k centroid shifts (12 distances) and half the distance to each centroid's nearest neighbour (66 distances). Result: 10,320,000 → 1,218,573 distances (88.2% cut, "strong"), 43 iterations, 84.0 s → 13.5 s.
R5: the per-point lower bound does the work: of 840,000 point-checks after iteration 1, 724,191 (86%) were skipped without any distance, 510,680 of those by the lower bound and 213,511 by the half-centroid-gap bound. Removing the lower bound left 7,424,326 distances (only 28% cut); removing the half-gap bound left 1,285,889. Its cost: every iteration n bound updates (no distances), and whenever a point fails the test a full k-distance scan to refresh it — those 71,624 full scans are 859,488 calls, 70.5% of my total.
SSE difference from the baseline: exactly 0 (10,737,882.94 both). It had to be: a point is skipped only when the triangle inequality proves its nearest centroid is unchanged, and full scans use the same tie rule, so every iteration has identical labels, identical means, and the same iteration count. The first iteration (a full scan, 240,000 distances) is 19.7% of my total.
